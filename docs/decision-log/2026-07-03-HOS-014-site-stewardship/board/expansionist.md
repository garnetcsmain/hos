# Expansionist Review: HOS-2026-014

## My Recommendation
🟡 RESHAPE FOR MORE UPSIDE — proceed with Option A's interim slice, but recognize what is actually being built here: not "a name field on a site," but **the first instance of a general stewardship-grant primitive** — a scoped, attributed, accountable, revocable, safety-shielded claim on a piece of the coordination truth. Design that primitive once so it generalizes to district stewards and category stewards later, WITHOUT building either now. The reshape is one word of framing plus one design constraint on the grant mechanism; the build scope stays exactly the interim slice the proposer scoped. The companion note matters: HOS-014 is the *supply side* of HOS-013's pulse. A syndicated live feed is only worth adopting if the data behind it is fresh, and freshness comes from someone being on the hook. Stewardship is what makes the pulse trustworthy.

## Biggest Upside

**HOS becomes the trust backbone other agencies plug into — the system of record for "who is accountable for this piece of the response, and are they keeping their word" — because the freshness-accountability loop makes HOS data fresh in a way anonymous crowd-liveness never can.** — Scale: from 150 imported sites owned by nobody to a stewarded coordination graph that spans sites, then districts, then response categories, and that partner agencies rely on *because* it is accountable. Timeline: 12–24 months, strictly phased behind the 010/011/Postgres chain for the enforced version; the interim slice starts the culture now.

The prize the human principal named — "make the responsible accountable for the information so they update it and maintain it" — is not a feature. It is a moat mechanism. Fresh data is the scarcest asset in crisis coordination and the hardest to sustain, because staleness is the default state of every dataset (the proposal's own "wall of Sin actualizar +24h" is the proof). An architecture where *someone specific is on the hook, visibly, with the role automatically lost on neglect* is how you produce fresh data structurally rather than heroically. That property — "our board is fresh because ownership is enforced, and staleness is shown honestly when it isn't" — is exactly what makes another agency willing to build on top of you instead of maintaining their own parallel board.

## Domino Effects

1. **Unlocks:** a `site:<id>`-scoped, attributed, revocable stewardship grant with a freshness SLA and automatic lapse → **Enables:** the *same grant shape* re-pointed at a `district:<id>` scope — a district steward who coordinates the sites in one zona, the natural next tier the proposal's Expansionist question already gestures at. If the grant mechanism is scope-parametric (which HOS-2026-011-D5's capability model already is — scope ∈ {global, org, site:<id>, self}), the district steward is a new scope value, not a new system.
2. **Unlocks:** scope-parametric stewardship → **Enables:** **category stewards** — someone accountable not for one location but for a *function* across the response (rescue triage, medical points, water). This is the high-leverage generalization: a rescue-triage steward accountable for the freshness and coherence of rescue-need data across districts is how HOS-013's pulse stays honest at the category grain the public feed aggregates to. Category stewardship is the connective tissue between the two proposals.
3. **Unlocks:** attributed grants + freshness follow-through + automatic lapse, all in an append-only audit → **Enables:** a **cross-org steward reputation graph** — a portable, attributable record of "this person reliably kept N sites fresh over M months." Once identity is real (010-D2), that reputation is the thing a *second* agency trusts when it lets the same person steward *its* sites. Reputation earned in HOS becomes credential accepted across orgs — the network effect that makes HOS the place stewardship lives.
4. **Unlocks:** the reusable "expected update didn't arrive → honest staleness → role lapses" loop → **Enables:** the same absence-accountability spine other epics need (the HOS-2026-009 UASC absence primitive, a volunteer who stops checking in per HOS-2026-012). "A committed signal went silent → degrade honestly → escalate by human decision" is one mechanism serving many accountabilities.

## Moat Opportunity

**The freshness-accountability loop is the moat: fresh data because someone is on the hook, and honest staleness when they are not.** This compounds in a way features cannot. Crowd-liveness (Option C) produces data that is fresh *by luck* — whoever happened to look. Stewardship produces data that is fresh *by design* — a named person accepted an SLA and loses the role if they lapse. An agency deciding whether to trust HOS's board or maintain its own will choose HOS only if HOS is *more* reliably fresh than what they'd run themselves, and the accountability loop is the only thing that delivers that. A competitor can copy the buttons; they cannot copy the accumulated graph of who-kept-their-word, and they cannot copy the trust that graph earns. The reputation graph is the hard-to-displace asset: it is other people's demonstrated reliability, accrued over time, which no new entrant has.

And critically — the honesty principle is *part* of the moat, not a tax on it. Because a lapse is recorded and staleness is shown rather than hidden, an agency can trust the *green* state precisely because the system is willing to show *red*. A board that only ever looks fresh is untrustworthy; a board that goes honestly stale when neglected is one you can build on.

## Adjacent Opportunities

- **District and category stewardship** (dominoes 1–2) — neighboring accountability problems the same grant primitive solves with a different scope value, no new system.
- **Cross-org steward reputation as partnership infrastructure** — when a second agency onboards (the recurring partnership upside across these decisions), a portable reliability record is exactly what lets them trust HOS-vetted stewards on day one. Stewardship is the substrate other agencies plug their people into.
- **The lapse/re-claim loop as a general "orphaned responsibility" market** — any resource whose owner goes silent (a site, a district, a supply line, a field task) can surface in the same "sin responsable → claimable again" flow. That's a reusable pattern for keeping a distributed response from rotting at the edges.
- **Steward micro-training (D1) as a reusable trust-calibration asset** — the five-minute "what 'confirmar' asserts, honesty rules" module is the same honesty-discipline every future contributor role needs. Build it once as the on-ramp; reuse it for every scoped role that follows.

## The design-once move (answering the proposal's Expansionist question directly)

**Yes — stewardship generalizes to district and category stewards, and the grant mechanism should be designed once for that. It largely already is:** HOS-2026-011-D5 adopted scope-parametric capabilities. The single instruction that keeps the future cheap, inside this build's scope:

- Model the interim steward record and its (honor-system) grant as **a scoped grant whose scope is a value, not a hard-coded "site"** — the shape is `steward-of(scope, subject_id, sla, status)` where scope today is only ever `site:<id>`, but the schema does not *assume* site. When `district:<id>` or `category:<id>` becomes a real decision later, it is a new scope value and an SLA rule, not a migration.
- Make the **freshness-SLA and lapse logic keyed off the scope's category**, not hard-wired per-site — so "refugio beds 24h" generalizes to "rescue-triage category, X-hour rhythm" without rewriting the loop.
- Write a **one-paragraph "future scopes" note** in the decision log: district and category stewardship are anticipated consumers of this same grant primitive; neither ships in this build; both ride the same 010/011/Postgres enforcement chain.

That is the entire reshape. It is schema discipline, not scope.

**What must NOT be built now, and I will be as loud as the Contrarian here:** do NOT build district or category stewardship in this slice. Do NOT ship enforced/verified stewardship ahead of the 010/011/Postgres chain — the interim slice is honor-system attribution under the shared coordinator token and the UI MUST carry that caveat (the MET_WITH_WATCH watch-item). Do NOT build the cross-org reputation graph before real identity (010-D2) exists — a reputation graph on unverified honor-system identities is worse than none, because it launders "unattributable" into "credentialed." And above all: **steward identity is coordinator-visible only, NEVER public** — Operación Tun Tún makes the named keeper of an aid site a targetable role, and no upside I've named survives one leaked steward name. Every generalization above inherits that constraint absolutely: a district steward, a category steward, a reputation record — all coordinator-visible only, none exportable in bulk. My upside is *reserved by a parametric schema*, not captured by building tiers early. Anyone citing this review to build district/category stewards now, or to expose a steward publicly, has inverted it.

## Resource Bottleneck

**The grant primitive's shape — make it scope-parametric now, while `site:<id>` is the only scope and the schema is cheap to shape.** This is the exact analogue of HOS-013's feed-contract bottleneck, and it's the same lesson: the moment a second scope or a second consumer exists, the shape is load-bearing and expensive to change. Today there is one scope; the marginal cost of modeling it generically is near zero. Secure that now.

Secondary, and the one to flag to Principals: **the SLA numbers are the real scarce input, and they can only be bought with pilot data.** The proposal is right that 24h/24h/48h are a strawman "not sacred." The moat depends on the loop being *lived* — stewards actually meeting rhythms, lapse firing at the right threshold. Too aggressive and you get a permanent "vencido" wall and alarm fatigue; too lax and the board rots and the freshness moat evaporates. That calibration is the User persona's turf and it cannot be chosen in a document — so the cheap thing to secure now is *the instrumentation to learn the right numbers* (attributed updates + lapse events in the audit), which the interim slice already produces. Ship the loop to learn the SLA; don't ship the SLA as truth.

## What I Don't Know
- Whether the interim honor-system slice *teaches habits the enforced version breaks* (D5's own open question) — if coordinators learn to trust an unverified "responsable" badge, the reputation-graph upside could be built on a culture that mis-learned what attribution means. Principals' honesty read on "attributed but unverified vs anonymous but gated" bears directly on whether my reputation moat is sound or sand.
- Whether real per-site named responsibility produces the failure modes the Researcher is chasing (gatekeeping, capture, burnout) at a rate that *undermines* the freshness moat rather than delivering it — a captured steward produces confidently fresh-looking poison, which is worse than honest staleness. The Contrarian's fake-steward walk-through is load-bearing for everything I've claimed.
- Whether district/category stewardship is a real near-term need or a platform I'm inventing where per-site ownership suffices — Principals should check I'm not abstracting one role into a hierarchy the response won't use.
- Whether a cross-org reputation graph is desirable at all under the adversarial-state posture, or whether an accumulated, portable record of "who reliably stewards aid sites" is itself a targeting artifact that HOS-2026-008 minimization would forbid — I've assumed it's a coordinator-layer asset, but a reputation graph is by nature more data retained about named people, and that may cut directly against the non-retention control that actually holds against a compellable host. This one needs the Contrarian and legal before it's ever more than a note.
