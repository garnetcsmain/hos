# Principals Review: HOS-2026-014

## My Recommendation

🟡 **RESHAPE.** The model is sound and, unlike its companion, it is *information*-first: it fixes a real data-freshness problem (150 anonymous stale cards) with an accountability loop rather than a new surface. It is also reversible by design (re-claimable sites, recorded lapses). But three elements as written cross a stated principle and must be reshaped before build:

1. **Auto-lapse as an *automatic* state change** brushes "AI recommends, people decide" and, worse, in this specific adversarial population it risks the system mislabeling a *detained or displaced* steward as a *negligent* one. Make lapse a coordinator action prompted by the triage list, not an automatic transition.
2. **Attributed-but-honor-system is better than anonymous-but-gated — but only conditionally,** and the condition (the unverified caveat rides at equal visual weight as the name, and public grain is role-not-name) is not yet a binding invariant. As framed it is a honesty hazard.
3. **The interim slice creates one concrete, cheap-to-prevent / impossible-to-fix-later debt** — an append-only audit log that cannot distinguish honor-era from enforced-era attribution — that MUST be closed on day one with a trust-tier flag on every write.

Per sub-decision: D1 🟡 (minimize the record), D2 🟡 (drop the self-request queue from the *interim*), D3 🟡 (lapse → human-in-loop), D4 🟢 (the safety rule is the correct resolution of the Tun Tun tension), D5 🟡 (ship interim, but with the trust-tier flag). Option A is the right shape; Options B and C are correctly rejected.

## Principles Alignment

- **AI recommends, people decide**: **VIOLATED by D3's automatic lapse, ALIGNED everywhere else.** Grants are coordinator-approved (no auto-grant — good, D2 is careful here). But "sustained neglect → stewardship LAPSES automatically" is an *automated, role-affecting action against a person driven by an inference* (silence ⇒ neglect). It is low-stakes and reversible, so this is a brush not a breach — but see Logic Check #2: silence is ambiguous in exactly this crisis, and a system that automatically strips the role of a steward who went silent because they were *detained* (Operación Tun Tun is cited in this very proposal) is inferring negligence from what may be victimization. The honest fact — "silent 7 days" — is already carried by the freshness badge. Let the coordinator decide what the silence means. Human-in-loop restores the principle at near-zero cost.

- **Information before interfaces**: ALIGNED, and this is the proposal's strength. It adds no new surface for the affected person; it makes *existing* data trustworthy by putting someone on the hook and showing staleness honestly when they are not. The 'sitios vencidos' triage list is information (a worked queue), not spectacle. This is the correct priority — and it is the freshness substrate its companion HOS-013 would display, which is the right dependency direction: fix the data before you animate it.

- **Trust & honesty layer**: **CONDITIONAL — this is the proposal's own Principals question, and the answer is "better, but only if the caveat wins the UX fight."** See Logic Check #1 for the full argument. The honesty risk is precise: rendering "confirmado por María" on a substrate (shared token, no real auth) that *cannot actually bind María to the act* is the same inversion the Board has policed since "stale must read as stale" — *unverified rendered as verified*. The proposal has the right instinct (the MET_WITH_WATCH honor-system caveat), but a name reads as truth and a caveat reads as fine print; the design must make the two equal weight or the honesty guarantee is nominal.

- **Data minimization / least-PII**: ALIGNED in framing, with **one pressure point the proposal under-weights.** D4 correctly limits the record to "name/handle, channel, site link — nothing else." But under HOS-008's own posture, the control that holds against the *compellable foreign host* is minimization, not access rules — and the single highest-risk field in the steward record is the **mandatory contact channel (phone/WhatsApp)**. A phone number is a strong, cross-referenceable identifier and a direct targeting vector; it sits in a compellable DB; "coordinator-visible only" and "no bulk export" do not protect it from the host-via-state adversary the proposal accepts as in-scope. Two consequences: (a) bias the record to a *handle*, not a legal name — the coordinator needs to *recognize* the steward, not hold their identity documents; (b) treat the contact channel as the record's crown-jewel PII and justify why it must be *stored* at all versus held out-of-band by the coordinator. Minimization says the real name and a persistent phone number may be more than the minimum the accountability purpose requires.

- **Auditability**: ALIGNED, and **internally consistent with "identity never public"** — this was one of the Board's questions and the answer is a clean yes. Auditability requires that every state change be *attributable to an accountable party in the append-only record*; it does **not** require that party be *publicly visible*. Attribution and public-visibility are orthogonal axes. So: attributed to the steward entry, visible to coordinators and the audit, never to the public/volunteer surface. That is fully consistent — and it is the *correct* resolution of the Tun Tun tension (public naming is lethal; coordinator-scoped attribution satisfies the audit). The one consistency gap is not in the logic but in the enforcement: "coordinator-visible + no bulk export" is a weak control against a *compromised* coordinator token (screen-scraping defeats no-bulk-export) and no control at all against the compellable host — which is why the minimization point above is load-bearing, not cosmetic. **Attributed to whom:** the vouched steward entry. **Visible to whom:** coordinators + auditors, at name grain; public + volunteers, at role grain ("responsable del sitio"). That is coherent.

- **Crisis-grade & reversible**: ALIGNED on reversibility (sites re-claimable, lapses recorded-not-hidden, revocation instant), **but auto-lapse cuts against "crisis-grade"** — the mechanism punishes exactly the crisis condition (a steward off-grid, displaced, or detained) it should tolerate. Reversible ≠ appropriate: the steward gets the role back, but the interim state falsely reads "abandoned" for someone who was disappeared. Human-in-loop lapse fixes this too.

## Logic Check

1. **Claim (the proposal's Principals question): "Is attributed-but-honor-system better than anonymous-but-gated, or worse?"** — **Better, conditionally — here is the argument.** *Anonymous-but-gated* (any coordinator presses the buttons; audit records the shared token) is *maximally honest* — it claims exactly what it knows ("a coordinator confirmed at T") — but it gives **no accountability loop**: you cannot lapse, credit, or hold responsible an entity that does not exist as a distinct party. It solves freshness *signal* and forfeits the entire *stewardship* purpose. *Attributed-but-honor-system* buys the accountability loop but risks over-claiming: "María confirmed" on a substrate that can't bind María. **The resolution turns on what the attribution actually asserts.** A coordinator-vouched steward entry *is a real thing the system honestly knows* — "coordinator X created and vouched for entry 'María'; grants flow to it." The honest claim is *"vouched by a coordinator, identity unverified, honor-system"*; the dishonest claim is *"verified: María López."* Since D4 already caps **public** grain at role-level ("responsable del sitio, hace 2h") — a claim the system *can* honestly back (a vouched responsible did act) — the public honesty exposure is bounded. The internal name carries the honor-system caveat. **Therefore attributed is better than anonymous *if and only if* the attribution claims only what it can back and the caveat rides at equal weight as the name.** Without that condition, it is worse than anonymous, because it manufactures unearned identity-confidence. The proposal has the condition in prose; make it a binding UI invariant.

2. **Claim (D3): "Sustained neglect (3 missed windows / 7 days silent) means the steward has abandoned the site; auto-lapse is the honest response."** — **Does not hold as an inference.** Silence ≠ abandonment, and in this population the confound is not hypothetical: displacement, network loss, device sharing, arrest. The proposal itself cites a *fatal* targeting precedent (Tun Tun) three sections earlier. So the system's own threat model says a steward may go silent because the adversary reached them — and D3 responds by automatically declaring them negligent and re-opening their site. The *honest* signal (silence) is already rendered by the freshness badge; auto-lapse layers an *inference* (neglect) on top of a *fact* (silence) and renders the inference as a state. That is the honesty principle inverted a second way. Resolve by keeping the honest staleness badge and making lapse a **coordinator action** off the 'sitios vencidos' list ("this steward has missed 3 windows — reassign or check on them?"). Same outcome for genuine abandonment; no false negligence for the disappeared; and it satisfies "people decide."

3. **Claim (D5): "The interim honor-system slice starts the stewardship culture; the question is whether it teaches habits the enforced version breaks."** — **It teaches one habit the enforced version breaks, and one debt the enforced version cannot unwind unless prevented now.** The broken habit: honor-era "confirmar" is casual and free; enforced "confirmar" is a cryptographically-bound assertion — the *same gesture* silently changes meaning. Loud caveats mitigate this. The *unwindable-only-if-prevented-now* debt is sharper and is a direct consequence of append-only auditability (Logic Check owns this because append-only means you cannot retroactively re-tag old records): the interim writes name-grade attribution onto an unverified substrate, and when 010/011 land, the log must distinguish *honor-era* from *verified-era* attribution — which is **impossible after the fact if the trust tier was not stamped on each write at authoring time.** See Technical Debt.

4. **Claim: "Interim reuses the coordinator gate, sites schema, and existing audited updateSiteCapacity — no blocking prerequisites."** — **Holds** against the brief's inventory (audited liveness actions, freshness badges, three-tier auth, site_grants schema provisioned). The interim is genuinely a field + queue + rhythm/lapse-logic + triage view on existing substrate. Good — which also means the *cost of doing it right* (record shaped as a future grant, trust-tier flag, human-in-loop lapse) is marginal, so there is no engineering excuse to ship the un-reshaped version.

## Simplest Solution

The stated end is: *fresh data because someone specific is on the hook, and honest staleness when they are not.* Occam's minimal version that achieves exactly that:

1. A **steward record on the site**, coordinator-vouched, shaped as the future `site:<id>` grant (grantee ref + scope + audit) even though unenforced — so enforcement is a *fill-in*, not a *migration*.
2. **Attributed liveness actions** (exist already) with a **trust-tier flag** (`honor` vs `verified`) on every write.
3. The **'sitios vencidos' triage list**, stalest-first, steward-or-"sin responsable" visible.
4. **Coordinator-driven lapse** from that list (not automatic).

**What that drops from the proposal's interim, and why:**
- **The self-request queue (D2b) — defer it from the *interim*.** Self-request is the entry point for the fake-steward attack the Contrarian is asked to walk; in the interim it adds the whole remote-claim-verification surface for marginal reach when coordinator-initiated grants (D2a) plus a coordinator-run out-of-band phone check already cover the remote path *without* an open queue. Remote stays first-class as a *verification path inside coordinator-initiated grants*, satisfying the principal's "remote must be first-class" direction, without standing up a public "claim this site" button before real auth exists to defend it.
- **Automatic lapse (D3) → coordinator action** (Logic Check #2).
- **Per-category SLA numbers baked into logic** — start with the single honest freshness badge; the strawman rhythms (24h/24h/48h) are, by the proposal's own admission, "for the review, not sacred," so do not encode them as automation until the pilot tunes them. This is a User-persona validation, and hard-coding un-validated thresholds into an auto-lapse is the worst of both.

**What is lost by simplifying:** self-serve remote claiming at internet scale, in the interim only. That is the exact capability that needs real auth + verification to be safe, so deferring it to the enforced phase is not a loss — it is correct sequencing.

## Technical Debt Created

- **THE sharp one — prevent now, unfixable later:** append-only + honor-era attribution means that unless every interim write carries a **trust-tier flag (`honor`/`verified`)**, the enforced-era log can never separate vouched-but-unverified records from cryptographically-bound ones. Append-only forbids retroactive correction *by construction*. This is nearly free to add now and permanently impossible to add later. Non-negotiable condition on D5.
- **Re-verification backlog (owned nowhere in the proposal):** every interim coordinator-vouched steward is an identity *not* bound to a device-key. When HOS-010 lands, each must re-verify to bind, or HOS carries a permanent tier of legacy-unverified stewards. Someone has to run that re-verification campaign; the proposal should name it as a cost of the interim, not discover it at enforcement.
- **Grant-shape debt (cheap to avoid):** if the interim "steward field" is modeled as a loose name field rather than in the shape of the adopted `site:<id>` grant (011-D5), the enforced version backfills/migrates instead of fills-in. Model the record as a grant from day one.
- **Habit debt (mitigated, not eliminated):** the "confirmar" gesture changes meaning at enforcement; the loud honor-system caveat is the right mitigation but does not fully erase the trained casualness. Acceptable if flagged.
- **Threshold debt (avoided by the simplification above):** auto-lapse thresholds tuned in a no-notification honor era will be wrong once real nudges exist and silence means more. Deferring auto-lapse sidesteps this entirely.

## Confidence Score

**0.84** — why not higher: the central call (attributed-honor-system is *conditionally* better than anonymous-gated) rests on a UX judgment — that an equal-weight caveat can actually hold against a prominent name — which is empirically contestable and belongs partly to the User persona; I can prove the *logic* of the honest-claim boundary but not that the interface will honor it under real use. And whether the contact-channel storage is truly necessary versus holdable out-of-band is a workflow question I am reasoning about from the adversarial posture, not from watching a coordinator verify a remote claim. The fake-steward attack's actual chokepoint is the Contrarian's to walk; I only assert that removing the interim self-request queue removes the cheapest path to it.

## What I Don't Know

- Whether the strawman rhythms (refugio 24h / médico 24h / acopio 48h) are realistic for a shared shelter phone on a real shift — decisive for whether any lapse logic (auto *or* coordinator-driven) fires as signal or as noise. User/pilot territory.
- Whether a coordinator will *actually* perform the out-of-band verification (call the site's listed phone) cheaply enough that it is a real control and not a rubber stamp — if not, the remote path's only real defense is theater. Contrarian's walk-through settles this.
- Whether the steward record can hold a *handle* and no legal name and still let a coordinator recognize/trust the right person — the minimization ideal depends on this being workable in practice.
- Whether the interim's "sin responsable" default (150 sites, none claimed at launch) is *worse* than today's honest staleness — i.e., does un-owned-and-stale read as more broken than un-owned-and-neutral? A framing/User question, but it bears on whether the interim's first impression helps or hurts trust before stewards accrete.
