# Researcher Review: HOS-2026-014

## My Recommendation
🟡 RESHAPE — the *shape* of the model (named accountable point per site, multiple stewards, attributed updates, automatic lapse, identity coordinator-only) converges on established humanitarian practice and is well-grounded. Build the interim slice. But three specifics need reshaping against the evidence: (1) the strawman freshness SLAs (24h/24h/48h) are **markedly more aggressive** than any real camp-management cadence I can find — expect burnout and a permanent "vencido" wall unless validated down; (2) the remote-claim verification paths (org vouches / existing steward confirms) are the **exact vector documented elite-capture and gatekeeper networks exploit** — coordinator approval does not neutralize capture, it just moves it; (3) "named per-site responsibility" is a documented burnout role in the closest comparable (HOT), which *supports* the multi-steward + auto-lapse design but means the interim single-named-responsible framing should not be the target end state. Companion note: HOS-014 is the freshness engine for HOS-013's board — if the SLAs are unrealistic, the pulse board animates a substrate that is stale-by-design.

## Comparable Systems

- **Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT) — validator / mapper roles** — the closest analogue to "named person accountable for a data unit." *What they chose:* two-step validation, escalation paths for poor validators, and named quality responsibility. *Outcome/failure:* burnout is an **explicitly recognized** problem — HOT built a training/mentorship program specifically "to have more leaders and less burnout"; a 2026 study of 4,619 projects / 1.4M microtasks found the atomized-ownership model produced "fragmented and inconsistent" output. Lesson for D1/D3: named single-point responsibility *does* burn out and *does* need succession + cross-training; HOS's multi-steward + auto-lapse is the right mitigation, but the role must be designed for turnover from day one, not treated as durable. ([HOT stories/burnout](https://stories.hotosm.org/humanitarian-openstreetmap-team/); [Problem-user escalation wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Humanitarian_OSM_Team/Working_groups/Data_Quality_Control_and_Assurance/Problem_User_Escalation); [Ochoa-Ortiz 2026, Transactions in GIS](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/tgis.70288)) [High confidence.]

- **Ushahidi — report verification / poisoned-data workflow** — *What they chose:* explicit verified/unverified tagging and guidance that platforms must "be sensitive to poisoned data — information posted to deliberately mislead." *Outcome/failure:* the binary verified/unverified label proved insufficient to capture trust/actionability, and deployments carried a heavy manual verification burden. Directly relevant to D2/D5: HOS's interim honor-system caveat ("attributed but unverified") is doing exactly the job Ushahidi's unverified tag did — and Ushahidi's experience says that label must be *loud and legible*, because users otherwise read attributed-as-verified. ([Ushahidi verification / ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/questioning-crowd-data-verification-challenges-humanitarians); [Information Verification for Humanitarians](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343626083_Information_Verification_for_Humanitarians_A_Critical_Review)) [High confidence.]

- **UNHCR/IOM CCCM — site focal points & committees** — *What they chose:* community focal points and *committees* (plural, with legitimacy structures) rather than a single named individual; regular consultation meetings; feedback/complaint channels as the accountability mechanism. In 2021, partners supported 780 committee members across 260 managed sites and facilitated 2,770 committee meetings. *Outcome:* this is the mature, working version of what HOS-014 proposes — and it validates **multiple stewards per site over a single owner**, and accountability-via-feedback-loop over accountability-via-shaming. HOS's D4 "recognition inside the coordinator layer, never public shaming" matches sector practice. ([UNHCR CCCM](https://emergency.unhcr.org/coordination-and-communication/cluster-system/camp-coordination-and-camp-management-cccm-cluster-iasc); [CM Toolkit — coordination](https://cmtoolkit.org/chapter/coordination/key-issues)) [Medium-high confidence.]

- **Somalia aid "gatekeepers" + elite-capture literature** — the documented failure mode of routing accountability through locally-vouched intermediaries. *Outcome/failure:* in Somalia, intermediaries **intercepted delivered aid and resold it for profit**; community-based targeting is captured by local elites who privilege relatives/friends, documented across China, Ethiopia, India, Malawi, Tanzania. Direct hit on D2's remote-verification design: "the site's org vouching" and "a named existing steward confirming" are precisely the self-perpetuating gatekeeper vectors this literature warns about. Coordinator approval mitigates *fabricated* claims; it does **not** mitigate *captured* ones where the vouching chain is itself the capture. ([Devex — Somalia gatekeepers](https://www.devex.com/news/the-gatekeepers-to-providing-aid-in-somalia-90754); [GSDRC — challenges for international aid](https://gsdrc.org/topic-guides/social-exclusion/lessons-and-tools/challenges-for-international-aid/)) [High confidence this is a real, named failure mode.]

- **Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) / Sphere** — *What it says:* Commitment to accountability to affected populations; organisations "regularly share relevant and timely information"; feedback/complaint mechanisms are the accountability substrate; data minimization and responsible information-sharing are explicit. *Relevance:* HOS-014's attributed-updates + freshness-honesty + minimized steward record (name/handle/channel/link only) are directionally CHS-compliant. The one gap CHS would flag: accountability in CHS runs *to the affected community*, not just up to coordinators — HOS's model is coordinator-facing, which is defensible under the state-adversary posture but is a narrower reading of "accountability" than the standard intends. ([CHS 2024](https://emergency.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2024-01/4.%20The%20Core%20Humanitarian%20Standard%20on%20Quality%20and%20Accountability.pdf); [Sphere/CHS](https://spherestandards.org/humanitarian-standards/core-humanitarian-standard/)) [Medium-high confidence.]

## Ground-Truth Data

- **Real freshness cadence is weekly-to-quarterly, not 24–48h.** Established CCCM demographic/site data is refreshed **monthly or quarterly**, with **weekly** site monitoring in active/acute contexts; the CCCM IMO sets "standard frequencies" per data type. The proposal's strawman — refugio 24h, médico 24h, acopio 48h — is 3–20× faster than the sector norm for *professionally staffed* sites. HOS's acopios in an acute urban crisis genuinely change faster than a stable camp, so *some* compression is defensible, but a 24h volunteer-maintained SLA is above what even paid CCCM operations sustain. Evidence strongly predicts the risk the proposal itself names: a permanent "vencido" wall and alarm fatigue. ([CCCM Cluster IM](https://www.cccmcluster.org/resources/coordination-toolkit/information-management); [Sites Population & Needs Monitoring reports](https://www.cccmcluster.org/resources/sites-population-needs-monitoring-01102025)) [Medium confidence — cadences vary by operation and I'm generalizing from cluster norms.]

- **Named-responsibility roles burn out — quantified indirectly.** HOT treats burnout as a first-order program risk (built mentorship to counter it); the microtask study shows atomized ownership degrades output quality at scale (4,619 projects). This is the best available evidence that HOS-014's core mechanism has a known decay curve and needs the succession machinery (multi-steward, auto-lapse, re-claimable) that the proposal, to its credit, already includes. [Medium-high confidence.]

- **Poisoned data is a named, planned-for threat in the closest system.** Ushahidi's own verification guidance calls out deliberate poisoning; HOS-014's Contrarian fake-steward attack is not hypothetical paranoia, it is a documented crisis-mapping failure mode. [High confidence.]

- **Steward-as-target is a real, fatal-in-context risk.** Reinforced by the brief's Operación Tun Tun precedent and the general finding that in authoritarian/hostile contexts, holding a named coordinating role over aid is targetable. D4 (identity coordinator-only, never public, no bulk export) is the correct and evidence-consistent control. ([HRW Rohingya data](https://www.hrw.org/news/2021/06/15/un-shared-rohingya-data-without-informed-consent) as the analogous "protection data → targeting" case) [High confidence on the direction.]

## Assumption Tests

| Coordinator out-of-band verification (org vouches / existing steward confirms) stops bad claims | **PARTIAL / CONTRADICTED for capture** | Stops fabricated claims; the vouching chain is the exact elite-capture/gatekeeper vector (Somalia; community-targeting capture across 5+ countries) |
| A named per-site responsible person is a durable, workable model | **PARTIAL** | Works with succession (CCCM committees, multi-steward); as a *single durable owner* it burns out (HOT) — supports multi-steward + auto-lapse, not single-named-owner as end state |
| Multiple stewards per site + automatic lapse is the right resilience design | **SUPPORTED** | CCCM uses committees, not individuals; HOT built mentorship/cross-training against burnout |
| "Attributed but unverified" honor-system caveat is meaningfully honest to users | **SUPPORTED, conditional** | Ushahidi verified/unverified tagging works only when the label is loud; attributed reads as verified unless the caveat is legible |
| Steward identity must stay coordinator-only under the state adversary | **SUPPORTED** | Operación Tun Tun; protection-data-becomes-targeting-data precedent (Rohingya) |
| Accountability-via-visible-follow-through-and-role-loss (not public shaming) matches practice | **SUPPORTED** | CHS/CCCM: accountability via feedback loops, not naming/shaming |
| Accountability is adequately served by a coordinator-facing loop | **PARTIAL** | CHS intends accountability *to affected people*; coordinator-only is a defensible narrowing under the adversary posture but is narrower than the standard |
| One-tap / WhatsApp-shaped update survives a real shelter shift on a shared phone | **UNKNOWN** | No direct evidence found; a User-member + pilot question, not settleable from precedent |

## Unknown Unknowns
- **The right SLA numbers for HOS's actual sites.** I can tell you 24h is too fast vs. the sector and 48h is aggressive; I *cannot* tell you the correct value for a Caracas centro de acopio in July 2026 — that depends on real turnover rates the pilot must measure. Recommend: ship with generous SLAs (or category-tuned, validated after 2–4 weeks of real steward data), and treat the strawman numbers as instrumentation targets, not thresholds, exactly as the proposal's own risk note hints.
- **Whether coordinators will actually *do* the out-of-band verification.** The Contrarian's real question. Precedent says verification burden is the thing that silently lapses under load (Ushahidi's moderation bottleneck). If the cheapest verification (call the site's listed phone) is skipped under pressure, capture and fabrication both walk through. This is behavioral and pilot-measurable, not precedent-answerable.
- **Shared-device attribution integrity.** On a shared shelter phone under one coordinator token, "attributed" updates may all resolve to the device, not the human. The honor-system caveat covers the *identity* gap but not the *which-of-several-shift-workers* gap. I found no comparable that solves attribution on a genuinely shared device pre-real-auth.
- **Capture-via-legitimate-affiliation.** The nastiest case the literature implies but I can't quantify: a real, correctly-affiliated steward whose *interests* are captured (skews a site "closed" to divert aid, or "open" as a honeypot). Verification confirms affiliation, not intent. No comparable fully closes this; audit + revocation + multi-steward cross-check is the realistic ceiling.

## Confidence Score
**0.75** — why not higher: the structural findings are solid and directly sourced — HOT burnout, Somalia gatekeeper capture, Ushahidi poisoned-data, CCCM committee/cadence practice, CHS accountability framing are all real and correctly named (high confidence). The soft spots are numeric and behavioral: the exact realistic SLA for HOS's sites and whether coordinators will sustain verification are pilot-measurable, not precedent-derivable, and I've marked them UNKNOWN rather than guess. The reshape is narrow and specific — validate SLAs down, harden verification against capture (not just fabrication), and frame the role as inherently turnover-prone — not a challenge to the model's core, which the evidence supports.
