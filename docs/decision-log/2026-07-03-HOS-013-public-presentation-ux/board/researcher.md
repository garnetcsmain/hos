# Researcher Review: HOS-2026-013

## My Recommendation
🟡 RESHAPE — D2 (Mi tablero), D3 (onboarding), D4 (Ver como interim toggle) and D5 (console ergonomics) are well-grounded and low-risk; build them. D1's coordinator-gated pulse board is fine. But the **public** D1 variant rests on one assumption the evidence actively contradicts: that a PII-free, k≥5, 15-minute-bucketed serializer neutralizes the targeting-oracle risk. It neutralizes *individual re-identification*; it does **not** neutralize the *aggregate-pattern* oracle ("which district is weakest right now"), which is precisely the signal the pulse board is designed to make legible. That is the same class of leak that burned Strava. The public flip must clear the 007 re-review on the strength of the *pattern* threat model, not the PII contract. Separately, the premise that an animated "live" board *builds* trust is unproven-to-contradicted by the dashboard literature — trust tracks provenance and honesty, not motion.

## Comparable Systems

- **Strava Global Heatmap (2018; Le Monde #StravaLeaks 2024–25)** — released aggregated activity data with individual PII stripped and points aggregated to the street grid. *Outcome/failure:* the **aggregate** movement pattern outlined undisclosed military bases in Syria/Afghanistan and, in the 2024–25 revival, tracked named security/military personnel and sensitive sites. The lesson is exact and load-bearing for D1: removing PII and coarsening does **not** remove an aggregate-pattern oracle when the pattern itself is the sensitive signal. ([Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/if-you-cant-take-the-heat-map-benefits-risks-of-releasing-location-datasets/); [Mapulus](https://www.mapulus.com/blog/strava-fitness-tracker-military-secrets-location-data); [AIIA](https://www.internationalaffairs.org.au/australianoutlook/strava-heatmap-exposes-military-bases/)) [High confidence this is the correct precedent.]

- **Ushahidi (Haiti 2010, Libya 2011)** — public crowdsourced crisis map. *Outcome/failure:* the small team could not keep pace with inbound volume; "suspicion of the crowd" and poisoned-data fear drove a heavy manual verification burden; independent evaluators noted the polished visual "casts an illusion of simplicity over a complex situation." Relevant to D1: a slick public feed raises expectations of completeness/accuracy it cannot meet, and the verification/moderation cost is the hidden line item. ([Ushahidi Haiti evaluation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265059793_Ushahidi_Haiti_Project_Evaluation_Independent_Evaluation_of_the_Ushahidi_Haiti_Project); [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/archive/2014/crowdsourcing-during-a-crisis-has-its-drawbacks/); [ReliefWeb — Questioning the Crowd](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/questioning-crowd-data-verification-challenges-humanitarians)) [High confidence.]

- **COVID-19 public dashboards (2020–22, 100+ studied)** — the closest large-N evidence on whether "live" real-time public dashboards help or hurt trust. *Outcome/failure:* dashboards "give a misleading impression of certainty… presenting cases and deaths as precise numbers even when the true numbers are unknowable"; 24.7% did not disclose their data source; evaluators found trust tracked **transparency, provenance and honest uncertainty**, not real-time-ness or visual polish. Directly tests the proposal's implicit "sci-fi live board → trust" thesis. ([JMIR Human Factors survey](https://humanfactors.jmir.org/2023/1/e43819); ["The Case Against Dashboards"](https://vdl.sci.utah.edu/blog/2020/07/06/dashboards/); [Western Cape dashboard evaluation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9797888/)) [Medium-high confidence.]

- **Rohingya registration data (UNHCR/Cox's Bazar, 2018+; HRW 2021)** — protection dataset on a persecuted population. *Outcome/failure:* data reached the government the population fled; the sector's own "worst-case scenario." The standing warning for any *public* HOS surface under a latent state adversary: minimize and gate, do not publish what a compellable/observing state can weaponize. ([HRW 2021](https://www.hrw.org/news/2021/06/15/un-shared-rohingya-data-without-informed-consent); [The New Humanitarian](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/opinion/2017/10/23/irresponsible-data-risks-registering-rohingya)) [High confidence.]

## Ground-Truth Data

- **k-anonymity is a floor, not a guarantee.** The literature is consistent: k-anonymity "does not guarantee privacy against attackers using background knowledge," and spatial-temporal k-anonymity has documented weaknesses against composition and *sequence-rule inference* attacks — i.e., an adversary who watches the coarsened feed *over time* can reconstruct sensitive patterns the single snapshot hides. A static k≥5 + 15-min bucket is a reasonable minimum but is **not** a proof of safety against a patient observer. ([PLOS ONE / PMC5540611](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5540611/); [ScienceDirect k-anonymity overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/computer-science/k-anonymity)) [High confidence on the direction; I cannot compute the residual risk for HOS's specific district cardinality — see Unknown Unknowns.]

- **Low-end Android / 2–3G reality supports snapshot-first.** Testing baselines default low-tier mobile to ~6× CPU slowdown and 3G-like networks; devices <2GB RAM are ~30% of the global market; CSS/JS animation is a documented battery and jank cost on old phones. The proposal's "static SSR snapshot first, animation as progressive enhancement" is the correct and evidence-backed default. ([CSS Wizardry — Low/mid-tier mobile 2025](https://csswizardry.com/2025/08/low-and-mid-tier-mobile-for-the-real-world-2025/); [web.dev mobile performance](https://web.dev/articles/mobile-optimization-and-performance)) [High confidence.]

- **WhatsApp OG-card constraints are concrete and favor a static payload.** The unfurled card is built server-side from `og:` tags: image ideally <600KB, the `<head>` must sit within the first ~300KB of HTML, page must be public + HTTPS + fast. The card *is* a static snapshot — which means the real distribution artifact is already the degraded, animation-free view. This validates designing the OG snapshot as the primary public object and the pulse animation as a secondary enhancement. ([Meta for Developers — Link Previews](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/link-previews/); [OpenGraphPlus WhatsApp guide](https://opengraphplus.com/consumers/whatsapp)) [High confidence.]

- **Real CCCM/crisis data cadence is slow.** Established camp-management demographic data is refreshed monthly or quarterly, with weekly *site monitoring* in active contexts (see the HOS-014 review). Implication for D1: a board that *animates* near-real-time state transitions will, most of the time, be animating a thin trickle of events over a mostly-stale substrate. The honesty risk is not fake motion (the proposal forbids that) but **implied freshness** — motion reads as "live everywhere" even where the underlying site data is days old. ([CCCM Cluster IM](https://www.cccmcluster.org/resources/coordination-toolkit/information-management)) [Medium confidence.]

## Assumption Tests

|---|---|---|
| A PII-free serializer (no names/free-text/precise locn, k≥5, 15-min buckets) makes the public feed safe | **CONTRADICTED (as stated)** | Strava: aggregation removed PII yet leaked sensitive sites via *aggregate pattern*; the "weakest district right now" signal is an aggregate-pattern oracle the PII contract does not touch |
| k≥5 + 15-min buckets neutralizes a district-weakness targeting oracle | **CONTRADICTED / UNKNOWN** | k-anonymity fails vs. background knowledge and temporal sequence-inference; residual risk depends on district cardinality + event rate, uncomputed here |
| An animated "live pulse" board increases outsider trust/adoption | **UNKNOWN, leans CONTRADICTED** | COVID-dashboard evidence: trust tracks provenance/honesty, not real-time animation; polish can manufacture false certainty |
| Static SSR snapshot degrade-path is the right primary for low-end phones | **SUPPORTED** | 6× CPU / 3G baselines; animation battery+jank cost; WhatsApp OG card is itself a static snapshot |

| Honesty principle (motion binds 1:1 to real events; demo labeled) is sufficient to protect trust | **PARTIAL** | Necessary but not sufficient — evidence says the residual honesty gap is *implied freshness* over a slow substrate, not fake motion |
| Demo-mode-on-landing (synthetic, labeled) doesn't pre-empt the gate | **UNKNOWN** | No external precedent found; this is a house judgment call for Principals, not a research-answerable question |

## Unknown Unknowns
- **The actual residual re-identification risk for HOS's district geometry.** k≥5 is a parameter, not a proof. I cannot verify whether Caracas-district cardinality + real event rate leave a weakest-district oracle exploitable at 15-min grain without an adversarial simulation on the real data. This is a Contrarian + engineering task (replay the real feed, run an inference attack), not something web precedent can settle. Treat the public flip as blocked on that simulation, not just on the serializer tests passing.
- **Whether the animated framing helps or hurts *this* audience specifically.** All my trust evidence is COVID/Global-North dashboards. I found no study on animated live-pulse boards with a Venezuelan crisis-affected + diaspora audience. The adoption-driver question (pulse board vs. Mi tablero + search) is genuinely open and should be settled by the User member and a moderated test, not asserted.
- **Verification/moderation load of a public feed.** Ushahidi's hidden cost was human moderation. HOS's public D1 is aggregate-only (no inbound public reports), which *avoids* most of that — but if the pulse board ever becomes a side door to inbound public content, the Ushahidi cost returns. Flag scope creep.
- **OG-card caching as a slow leak.** WhatsApp/Meta cache unfurled cards; a card scraped once can persist and be re-shared after the underlying data changes or is retracted. Minor, but it means "deletable in one tap" (D2) does not reach an already-unfurled card. Not researched deeply.

## Confidence Score
**0.74** — why not higher: the two most decision-relevant findings are asymmetrically solid. The Strava pattern-oracle precedent and the k-anonymity limits are strongly and directly sourced (high confidence). The trust claim rests on COVID-dashboard analogues, not on a same-context study (medium). And the one number that would actually gate the public flip — the residual oracle risk at HOS's real district cardinality — I cannot compute from precedent; it needs an adversarial simulation. Every low-risk sub-decision (D2–D5) is well-supported; my reshape is aimed squarely at the public D1 justification.
